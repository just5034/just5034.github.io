# Job Search Playbook

Strategy for converting skill + portfolio into a first cybersecurity offer. Start executing mid-Phase 3 (October 2026).

---

## Target Roles

Ranked by fit for Justin's profile:

### Tier 1 — Best fit
1. **AI Red Team / AI Security Engineer** — frontier labs, AI security startups
   - Unique ML depth + offensive skill = rare combination
   - Target: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Meta AI red team, Microsoft AI Red Team
   - AI security startups: HiddenLayer, Robust Intelligence, Protect AI, Lakera, Haize Labs
   - Salary range: $120-180k for first role, often higher
2. **Application Security Engineer (AppSec)** — shifts left into dev, reviews code, threat models
   - Natural fit for someone who can code
   - Target: mid-size tech companies, fintech, healthtech
   - Salary range: $100-140k in SoCal / remote

### Tier 2 — Solid fit
3. **Junior Penetration Tester** — consulting firms or internal teams
   - OSCP is table stakes
   - Target: Bishop Fox, NCC Group, Mandiant, Optiv, smaller boutiques, corporate internal red teams
   - Salary range: $75-110k in SoCal
4. **Detection Engineer** — writes detection rules, hunts threats
   - ML background is a strong asset
   - Target: tech companies with mature security orgs, MDR providers
   - Salary range: $90-130k

### Tier 3 — Bridge roles if needed
5. **Security Analyst / SOC Analyst** — alert triage, IR support
   - Fastest to hire, lowest ceiling
   - Target: any company with a SOC — MSSPs, mid-market enterprise
   - Salary range: $60-85k SoCal
6. **Security Engineer (generalist)** — implements controls, works across stack
   - Broader role, good if offensive track doesn't click
   - Salary range: $90-130k

## SoCal Company Landscape

### Defense and government contractors (abundant in SoCal)
- Northrop Grumman, Raytheon, Lockheed Martin, Aerospace Corp, Boeing, RAND
- Require US citizenship, often clearance-eligible
- Pay is solid, training is good, pace is slower than tech
- Good entry if you're open to clearance-track work

### Tech companies with SoCal presence
- Snap, SpaceX, Riot Games, Activision, Disney (entertainment cyber), Hulu
- Smaller orgs with security teams: Whisper, ServiceTitan, GoodRx

### AI/ML-adjacent
- Many frontier AI labs are remote-first — your SoCal location doesn't limit you
- Target remote roles aggressively; that's where AI red team jobs often are

### Consulting / assessment firms
- Bishop Fox, NCC Group, Mandiant (Google Cloud), Optiv, Praetorian, TrustedSec
- Often hire junior pen testers with OSCP + strong portfolio
- Good training ground, grind hours

## Resume / LinkedIn Positioning

### Core pitch (one sentence)
"Security engineer with deep ML/AI research background, offensive security skill (OSCP), and demonstrated work at the intersection of AI systems and security."

### Resume structure (for cyber applications)
1. **Name, contact, links**: blog, GitHub, HackerOne, LinkedIn
2. **Summary**: 2-3 sentences leading with security skill and AI background as differentiator
3. **Certifications**: OSCP prominently, Security+ if obtained
4. **Security projects and portfolio**: put this BEFORE work experience since it's the relevant evidence
5. **Work experience**: frame prior ML roles with security-adjacent achievements where honest ("built systems handling sensitive data," "implemented auth," etc.)
6. **Education**: MS Data Science Columbia
7. **Publications**: AI/ML papers — shows research depth

### What to de-emphasize
- Unrelated CTO-at-Hovian work (keep brief)
- Genomics / whale audio specifics unless directly relevant
- GPA

### LinkedIn tweaks
- Headline: "Offensive Security | AI Security | MS Data Science @ Columbia"
- About: lead with security transition narrative
- Add OSCP as certification once earned
- Feature portfolio links

## Application Strategy

### Channels, ranked by ROI
1. **Referrals** — from meetup contacts, DEF CON connections, former colleagues
2. **Direct outreach** — cold email a security engineer at a target company, ask for info/referral
3. **Company career sites** — direct applications
4. **LinkedIn Easy Apply** — low effort, low conversion; do in batches
5. **Specialty job boards**: InfosecJobs, CyberSecJobs, ClearanceJobs (for cleared roles)
6. **Recruiters specializing in security** — let them find you on LinkedIn

### Volume target
- 50-100 applications over Phase 3-4
- Target 10-15 active conversations/pipelines
- Expect 5-10% response rate on cold applications
- Expect 30-50% response rate on referrals

### Application cadence
- 5-10 applications/week during active search
- Custom cover letter only for top-tier targets; otherwise don't waste time on covers

## Interview Prep (Phase 4)

### Typical security interview loop
1. **Recruiter screen** — basic fit, expectations, salary
2. **Hiring manager screen** — experience, interest, why security
3. **Technical screen** — live exercises or knowledge check
4. **Onsite / final loop** — 3-5 technical rounds, often including practical exercise

### Topics to prepare
- **OWASP Top 10** — know cold, with real examples of each
- **Exploitation fundamentals** — SQLi, XSS, SSRF, auth bypass, privesc
- **Networking** — TLS handshake, DNS, HTTP methods, common protocols
- **Cryptography basics** — symmetric vs asymmetric, hashing, common mistakes
- **Secure coding** — common vulnerability classes in code review
- **Incident response basics** — even for offensive roles
- **Threat modeling** — STRIDE, attack trees

### Practice formats
- **HackTheBox interviews prep section** (if available)
- **Practice live hacking** on HTB in front of a webcam — simulate screen-share pressure
- **Mock code reviews** on intentionally vulnerable repos (DVWA, Juice Shop, WebGoat)
- **Practice verbalizing your thought process** — security interviewers want to see methodology

### AI-specific interview topics (if targeting AI security roles)
- Prompt injection attack vectors and defenses
- LLM agent system attack surface
- Training data poisoning, model extraction
- OWASP LLM Top 10
- MITRE ATLAS framework
- Have opinions on current hot topics (AI agent security, tool use risks)

## Salary Negotiation

### Know your numbers (SoCal 2027)
- Floor: $75k (don't go below unless it's a dream role with clear growth)
- Target: $90-110k
- Stretch: $120-150k+ for AI-angle roles

### Negotiation principles
- Never give a number first — "I'm targeting market rate for this role based on my background"
- Have competing offers if possible — apply broadly to create leverage
- Negotiate total comp, not just base — equity, signing bonus, PTO
- **Ask for 10-15% above first offer** — almost always acceptable
- Don't over-negotiate a role where you have genuine interest and reasonable comp

### What to negotiate besides base
- Signing bonus (often $5-15k at entry level)
- Equity (at startups)
- Remote work flexibility
- Training budget (including conferences, certs)
- Start date

## Red Flags to Avoid

- **Roles that promise training but don't specify any** — often means you'll be grunt-worked
- **"Must do 20 certs in first year" roles** — mill, not career
- **Unlimited PTO policies with no team norms** — often means less PTO taken
- **Glassdoor reviews mentioning burnout/24-7 on-call** for junior roles
- **Cleared roles when you're not serious about staying in that track** — clearances create golden handcuffs

## When Things Aren't Working

### If no responses after 30 applications
- Portfolio is probably weaker than you think — get feedback from mentors
- Resume may be too ML-heavy — lean harder into security framing
- Consider expanding to Tier 3 bridge roles

### If getting interviews but not offers
- Technical interview skills likely need work — do more practice loops
- Consider a mock interview service

### If the offensive track isn't landing by end of November 2026
- Pivot to detection engineering applications (Phase 2 backup plan)
- Consider SOC bridge role with clear 12-month pivot path

## Key SoCal Contacts to Build Before Applications

Use Phase 2 (summer) to develop 5-10 real relationships. Priorities:
- 1-2 senior pen testers at SoCal consulting firms
- 1-2 AppSec engineers at SoCal tech companies
- 1-2 hiring managers in the field
- 1-2 successful recent career-switchers (for tactical advice)
- 1-2 DC949/OWASP LA organizers (community credibility)

Most productive format: coffee chats (in person), 30 minutes, come with specific questions, follow up with thanks + update 2-3 months later.
